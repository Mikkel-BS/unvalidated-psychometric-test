# Literature-informed design review

Reviewed 25 September 2026. Questionnaire **0.2.0**, repository commit `5dfc3bf37aed01a101bfc2fcec6589dee91659e7`.

## What this review establishes

The earlier `QA.md` documents editorial and software checks. It was **not** a literature-based academic review. This document adds a structured review of the current 12 constructs, all 72 items, the score calculation, and the interpretations, informed by six research publications listed below.

This is an AI-assisted content and design audit, not independent academic peer review, a systematic literature review, an expert-panel content-validity study, or empirical validation. The item judgments and proposed rewrites below are my assessments of this particular questionnaire; the cited researchers did not study or endorse it. Candidate rewrites are proposals, not items already installed in the live test.

**Overall judgment:** the questionnaire is a plausible, openly experimental conversation game. Several scales have reasonably coherent content. It is not yet a well-developed psychological measure awaiting only a final validation stamp: some definitions and items still need refinement. Familiar trait names and correct arithmetic do not establish what the resulting scores measure.

## Research used and its relevance

1. **Clark & Watson (2019), _Constructing validity: New developments in creating objective measuring instruments_.** The paper emphasizes precise construct definitions, boundaries with nearby constructs, careful item pools, and evidence beyond internal consistency. It treats validity as support for particular interpretations and uses. Application here: specify what each score claims before polishing its narrative. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0000626) · [Paper](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/528Readings/ClarkWatson2019.pdf)
2. **DeYoung, Quilty & Peterson (2007), _Between facets and domains: 10 aspects of the Big Five_.** Their findings support correlated distinctions within broad personality domains, including enthusiasm/assertiveness and industriousness/orderliness. Application here: splitting social and work-style themes is plausible, but their evidence does not establish our particular subdivisions. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.93.5.880) · [Paper](https://emilywilloughby.com/Papers/DeYoung_2007_BFAS_JPSP.pdf)
3. **Soto & John (2017), BFI-2 development paper.** The authors developed a hierarchical inventory through conceptual work, item selection, and validation studies. Their successful use of content-balanced keying is a counterweight to a blanket rejection of reversed items. Application here: our equal keying is defensible as a hypothesis, not demonstrated bias control. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000096) · [Author-uploaded paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295563312_The_Next_Big_Five_Inventory_BFI-2_Developing_and_Assessing_a_Hierarchical_Model_With_15_Facets_to_Enhance_Bandwidth_Fidelity_and_Predictive_Power)
4. **van Sonderen, Sanderman & Coyne (2013), _Ineffectiveness of reverse wording of questionnaire items_.** In a fatigue questionnaire, reversing items did not prevent response bias and introduced problems consistent with confusion/inattention. Application here: mathematical reversal must not be mistaken for successful psychological measurement. This study does not show that every balanced personality scale is defective. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0068967)
5. **Suárez-Álvarez et al. (2018), _Using reversed items in Likert scales: A questionable practice_.** Their repeated-measures self-efficacy study found worse psychometric properties for a mixed wording form and raised comprehension concerns. Application here: check how friends interpret reversals, especially negations and qualified statements. The findings concern another construct and language, so direct effects on our English items remain unknown. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7334/psicothema2018.33) · [Paper](https://digibuo.uniovi.es/dspace/bitstream/handle/10651/48979/Using%20.pdf?sequence=1)
6. **Israelashvili, Sauter & Fischer (2019), _How well can we assess our ability to understand others' feelings?_** Across six studies, self-reported perspective taking and emotion-recognition performance had a modest positive association, approximately r = .20. Self-report was therefore not equivalent to tested skill. Our Emotional Attention items differ from theirs; the transferable caution is about the inference, not an expected correlation for our scale. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02475)

These sources support design principles and comparisons. **None supplies a reliability estimate, norm, factor loading, or validity coefficient for this questionnaire.**

## Construct map

The comparisons below are conceptual hypotheses informed by the personality taxonomies in sources 2 and 3, not equivalent scales or validated conversions. “Clearer” means clearer content in this item set, not higher measured reliability.

| Our scale | Plausible conceptual neighbourhood | Judgment about this item set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social Energy | Sociability / enthusiasm | Relatively clear. Mixes seeking company and recovery after company; those need not coincide. |
| Social Boldness | Assertiveness / social confidence | Plausible. Distinguish comfort speaking from wanting influence. One item drifts into willingness to admit uncertainty. |
| Influence | Assertiveness / dominance motivation | Plausible motivation scale. Some items still ask about confidence or perceived success. Distinctness from Boldness is untested. |
| Willingness to Compromise | Accommodation within agreeableness | Narrower label helps. Preferences, factual disagreements, and moral commitments must not be treated as interchangeable. |
| Emotional Attention | Self-reported attention to interpersonal cues | Weakest inference boundary: noticing, looking for cues, and being correct are different. It is not a broad empathy scale. |
| Structure | Organisation / orderliness | Relatively clear. Memory aids and detailed planning are different manifestations; planning does not establish dependable execution. |
| Persistence | Industriousness / sustained effort | Relatively clear. Boredom, obstacles, and long time horizons are different challenges. This is not all of conscientiousness or grit. |
| Adaptability | Comfort with change, plus possible openness and stress-reactivity content | Needs a narrower definition. Emotional irritation and practical adjustment can coexist. |
| Analytical Curiosity | Intellectual curiosity / engagement with reasoning | Relatively clear as an interest. It does not measure intelligence, correct reasoning, or absence of intuition. |
| Imagination | Exploration of ideas / creative imagination | Plausible but selective. Mainly speculative interests; excludes much aesthetic openness and does not measure imagery vividness. |
| Decisiveness | Commitment pace under uncertainty | Highest-priority coherence issue: information search, choice speed, post-choice doubt, and sensible action under time cost are mixed. |
| Emotional Steadiness | Lower negative emotionality / reactivity | Broad mixture of frustration, rumination, and recovery. Acceptable as an informal umbrella, not a narrow single-process claim. |

This is a selective profile, not comprehensive Big Five, HEXACO, OPQ, or occupational coverage. It cannot support a conversion to those instruments. There is no basis for adding a total “personality quality” score or treating the four display groups as empirically established factors.

## Scoring and response-format assessment

These are deductions from this project's code and item wording, not empirical findings about participants.

- **The arithmetic is transparent and defensible as a descriptive index.** Equal weighting avoids invented calibration. It assumes that combining these items is useful; it does not establish that assumption. A 0–100 rescaling adds no information and does not create an interval measure of an underlying trait.
- **There are 25 attainable score values, not 101 levels of known precision.** Six items scored 1–5 provide totals from 6 through 30. One raw response step changes the unrounded score by 25/6, about 4.17 points. This is score granularity, not a standard error or confidence interval.
- **The display thresholds are arbitrary.** With the actual rounding, 38 versus 42 changes the low/middle narrative and 58 versus 63 changes the middle/high narrative. Nothing establishes a meaningful psychological boundary there.
- **Six items are not automatically inadequate or adequate.** They are a practical length choice. Near-duplicates can leave a six-item set with narrow coverage; adding more similarly worded items would not necessarily fix that.
- **Balanced keying has a plausible purpose and a real tradeoff.** Sources 3–5 do not warrant either “three reversed items makes a scale rigorous” or “all reverse scoring is bad.” In our test, polarity also partly tracks content: for example, Social Energy's reverse items emphasise solitude/recovery while its positive items emphasise company/stimulation. Any future apparent wording effect would need to be distinguished from that content difference.
- **Five agreement options are a reasonable game format, not an empirically optimised choice here.** Mandatory responses offer no distinct “cannot judge / no relevant experience” option. Neutrality, uncertainty, and context dependence may therefore be mixed. A future optional unscored “cannot judge” choice would need an explicit missing-score rule; it must not be silently counted as 3.
- **Identical-answer warnings should remain descriptive.** Cancellation is mathematical. It does not diagnose carelessness, deception, or the person's actual balance. The current corrected language mostly respects that distinction.
- **No normative or between-scale comparisons are justified.** People can compare what they endorsed for discussion, but a ten-point difference is not an established psychological distance. No empirical basis exists for reporting confidence intervals, response-validity scores, inferred Big Five scores, or job predictions.

## Interpretation audit

The current results are substantially better than the initial sliders: they identify the limits, show the contributing answers, and avoid personality types and rank-based claims. Several boundaries still deserve care:

1. **Descriptions should follow the scored content.** Emotional Attention currently concerns reported noticing and focus; it should never become a claim about empathy skill, kindness, or correct mind reading. The distinction is consistent with source 6, though our scale is not the one tested in that paper.
2. **A midpoint does not reveal why answers average out.** The current explanatory note says this correctly, but some preceding trait-specific middle paragraphs still suggest context flexibility. For Social Boldness, for example, greater confidence in familiar settings was not directly established by a midpoint. A neutral statement about the answer average should precede any suggested explanation.
3. **Tradeoffs are discussion prompts, not additional measured findings.** A high Influence score does not demonstrate excessive airtime; high Structure does not demonstrate procrastination through planning. The “possible tradeoff” label helps, but these implications should remain explicitly hypothetical.
4. **Do not invent a strength to soften every low score.** Low persistence does not establish good judgment about quitting. The current softened wording is better than praising low effort as strategic flexibility. Equally, high scores need not be treated as problems waiting to happen.
5. **A low score is not always evidence of a positive opposite.** Low analytical interest is not intuitive talent; low attention to subtle cues does not establish clear communication. Current endpoint corrections appropriately avoid several of these leaps.

## Most useful next revision

**First priority: settle definitions, especially Decision Pace, Adaptability, and Emotional Attention.** For example, decide whether Decisiveness means speed of commitment in ordinary decisions or freedom from doubt afterward. Our six items should not silently combine these because both sound desirable. Keep comfort with change separate from never feeling annoyed by change. Keep attending to cues separate from correctly detecting another person's feelings.

Then test candidate language with friends before another wholesale rewrite. These are original proposals illustrating concrete fixes, not validated replacements:

| Current ID | Problem addressed | Candidate wording |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 18 | Self-defining “too long” | “I have little interest in exploring imaginary scenarios.” |
| 22 | Irritation treated as poor adjustment | “When plans change unexpectedly, it takes me time to settle into a new approach.” |
| 36 | Sensible action contrasted with endless delay | “For routine choices, I usually commit before I have explored every option.” |
| 48 | Post-choice rumination mixed with choosing | “For routine choices, I often leave the decision open while I compare alternatives.” |
| 55 | Admitting misunderstanding mixed with unfamiliar-group confidence | “I am comfortable speaking up in a group I have only just joined.” |
| 9 | Persuasive success mixed with motivation | “I enjoy trying to persuade a group to choose the option I favour.” |

Some replacements resemble other current items. They would need to be considered as a set, not automatically inserted one by one. A cleaner sentence is not proof of better discrimination or reliability.

**For a friends-only game, the best next evidence is modest and concrete:** ask a few people individually to paraphrase the items, explain what example they used, and identify wording they answered differently than intended. Compare those explanations before showing the intended scale. That can uncover misunderstandings and context differences; it cannot validate the factor structure.

If a more scientific hobby project is later desired, freeze a version, obtain informed participation, collect item-level responses and repeats, and investigate score distributions, item relationships, temporal stability, and overlap with established measures. Sample planning depends on the proposed analyses; a small friend group cannot establish a 12-factor model. Agreement with a friend is interesting feedback, not an objective answer key.

## Practical conclusion

I would use this version for curiosity and conversation with friends. I would describe it as **an original, literature-informed personality game with untested scales**. I would not call it academically validated, academically approved, or equivalent to an established inventory. The clearest remaining improvements are narrower definitions and less leading items, especially in Decisiveness and Adaptability—not more elaborate scoring or more confident result prose.
